Design language for the post-app era

Context Grammar

A framework for AI that understands human intent, reads the situation, uses memory responsibly, and translates context into the right action at the right moment.

01 Intent is the want, not just the text of a request.
02 Signals & Dials and Brain make context inspectable before action.

Why it matters

Human life is not a task queue.

The same AI action can be helpful, rude, risky, or dignity-preserving depending on the moment.

A reminder at 7am may be useful. The same reminder at 11pm after lights out may be invasive. A purchase under one household rule may be safe; under another, it should ask first. A family member may share schedule context but not health context.

Context Grammar asks what AI needs to understand about a human situation before it earns the right to act. It turns that question into a readable pipeline for product, design, and engineering decisions.

⇒ Inflow · Human

The system starts before the prompt.

Context Grammar begins with a person in a situation: walking, tired, watched by others, holding something, under time pressure, or managing a relationship.

In a restaurant, the first design object is not the order. It is the guest in context. The same pasta order means something different for a rushed commuter, a parent with a child, or a couple celebrating an anniversary.

01 · Intent v2

Intent is what the human is trying to accomplish.

Intent is not just the sentence a user says. It is the aim behind the moment. It may be explicit, active, passive, or ambient — and it can be inferred from Context Token × Brain intersection without a single word spoken.

In a restaurant, Intent is the reason behind the order. "Pasta" is the literal request, but the real intent may be to eat quickly, avoid an allergy, keep the conversation private, or make the meal feel special.

Read Intent
02 · 6 Situation Signals

Situation signals turn the situation into readable variables.

Six situation signals describe what is true right now: Physical State, Cognitive Load, Social Exposure, Priority Weight, Form Factor, and Feasibility.

In a restaurant, Situation signals are what a skilled waiter notices: date, business meal, birthday, first visit, rushed lunch, quiet table, wine preference, or non-alcoholic request.

Read Situation Signals
03 · 2 Relationship Dials

Relationship dials set what AI may know and do.

Disclosure controls what the AI may know. Autonomy controls how far it may act. These two relationship dials set guardrails before memory and decision logic run.

In a restaurant, a regular can ask for recommendations only because the restaurant knows allergies, preferences, budget, and boundaries. Trust is earned permission, not convenience.

Read Relationship Dials
04 · Rule Engine

Rules turn state into a UI command.

Situation signals, relationship dials, and Brain references are observations. The Rule Engine turns them into decisions a surface can render: reduce detail, ask first, hide sensitive data, switch to voice, or act.

In a restaurant, the Rule Engine is service judgment made explicit: confirm expensive choices, prioritize allergies, shorten the path for rushed guests, and escalate risk.

Read Rule Engine
05 · Negotiation Gate

The Gate evaluates before every action.

Before any agentic action is taken, the Negotiation Gate scores Confidence × Risk × Reversibility × Sensitivity. The result is either a silent proceed or a specific UI primitive — Assumption Cards, Interpretation Preview, or a block.

In a restaurant, the Gate is the waiter's judgment before bringing an expensive bottle: is the guest certain, is the risk real, and can it be returned? Only then does service proceed.

Read Negotiation Gate
06 · Autonomy Resolution

Autonomy Resolution

Final Autonomy = min(User Autonomy Setting, Gate Autonomy Ceiling). The core safety formula — a user-set "Auto" can still be downgraded to "Confirm" for high-risk actions.

In a restaurant, this is the moment where the kitchen overrides a guest's self-service request: "You asked to plate it yourself, but it's still hot — we'll bring it."

Read Autonomy Resolution
07 · AX × Lifecycle

Patterns make AI behavior reusable across the full lifecycle.

The resolved command becomes a pattern: delegate, escalate, adapt, delay, confirm, hide, recombine, or hand control back. These reusable moves span the entire lifecycle — from first engagement through monitor, adapt, and agentic response.

In a restaurant, AX Patterns are the repeated service moves: refill quietly, ask before changing spice, simplify the explanation, or bring a manager in.

Read AX × Lifecycle

Always-On Layer / 常に働くレイヤー

Three concepts that span the entire pipeline.

Memory, long-term trust, and moment-to-moment meaning alignment are always running, always referenced, always updated — they don't sit on a single stage.

Always-On · Memory

Brain

Three-layer memory: Identity (who you are), Learning (what you've taught the system), Now (what's happening this moment). Referenced by every stage. Updated during Monitor and Adapt.

Trust Design
