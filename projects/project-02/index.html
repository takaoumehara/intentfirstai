Intro & Hypothesis

Meeting the Sterns & The Disposable Brain

The Stern family of Brooklyn (Rachel, David, Eli, Maya) went to Japan for two weeks. Rather than polluting their permanent Home Brain with ephemeral details like currency conversions and train platform numbers, the system forks a temporary Disposable Brain.

David & Rachel (Adults)

Managing logistics, coordinating sibling equity, reviewing recommendations. Autonomy bounds = Confirm Mode.

Eli (13) & Maya (9) (Kids)

Experiencing anchors (e.g. Kameoka sword forge, gold leaf workshop). Telemetry feeds children's shiori dashboards.

Lifecycle

A Brain that knows it is temporary.

Four stages: Inherit baseline rules, accumulate local telemetry, distill findings back to the Home Brain, and dissolve the remaining details safely.

Stage 01
Birth (Brooklyn)

Home Brain forks baseline parameters (allergies, names) on travel eve. Excludes daily noise.

Stage 02
Learn (Japan)

Brain observes local data (Maya's 90-min focus, Eli's early skips) to optimize daily schedule.

Stage 03
Return (Brooklyn)

learnings (e.g. "Eli is a maker") merge back into Home Brain's Identity Layer.

Stage 04
Dissolve

Ephemeral details (hotel reservations, transport passes) expire and dissolve after 30 days.

Trust Temporal Arc

Care Contract

The 2+2 Sibling Balance Contract

Equality is not a feeling. It is a design parameter.

To prevent parent burnout and sibling friction, the Trip Brain manages a 2+2 Sibling Contract: each child receives equal anchor experience hours and individual narrative weight.

2+2 Constraint Check: Passed. Eli (22.0h) vs Maya (21.5h) total anchor hours.

Autonomy dial downgraded to Confirm Mode.

Autonomy Downgrade: Auto-booking disabled. Awaiting explicit confirmation.
